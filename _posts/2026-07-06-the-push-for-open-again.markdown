So none other than Alex Karp, the CEO of Palantir (creator of the forward deployed engineering concept) is now calling out the frontier labs for charging too much to use their models, and pushing the case for open models, such as the Nemotron family from Nvidia. Of course given all their defence, intelligence agency work they can't be seen to be pushing the chinese open models and so they are partnering with Nvidia. This all well and good but this inevitably leads us back to the economics. Much as opensource software isnt a business model, neither are open (weights) LLMs. If the frontier labs cant make a profit on their efforts who is going to pay for model development? And if they go public the question of profitability becomes more and more important of course. 

In the case of open model development who is paying the bills to train the models? Nvidia can afford to have that as part of their business model to incent more inference which will potentially run on their chips. ie its part of them being vertically integrated. But its hard to see how anybody else can justify it. Even in the case of google with their gemma models, or openai with their open models the incentives there arent economic but rather to deflect other pressures. In the case of the chinese companies it seems its a strategic investment in supply chain control, ultimately supported by government strategy. But its pretty clear that is a hook them and reel them in approach. Not an open forever model. There are a few institutions, eg Allen Institute, and educational institutions that will likely be open for the long haul, but otherwise i have a hard time seeing where the funding comes from to support an open approach long term, especially given the costs in training frontier models.

So we can all be concerned about the costs, but the market will decide on the cost of the supply by regulating the demand. Not be trying to work around the edges with "open" models from vendors that are incented to sell more chips. Or assuming currently open models will be open forever. I think that is naive.
