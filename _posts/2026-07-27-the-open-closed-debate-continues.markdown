Well this last week has been lots of fun. First we had OpenAI disclosing that a couple of their frontier models broke out of their constraints and attacked HuggingFace, using new zero days. And then HuggingFace couldnt defend themselves with proprietary models because of the cyber self-censoring and so had to turn to a chinese open model for help. You can imagine how all that played out in the open vs closed debate. Now its not just cost and choice but also cybersecurity at play.

The big players with their enlightened self-interest showing penned an open letter on Friday calling for AI to be open, followed by a new organization being formed today "Open Secure AI Alliance for AI Safety and Security" pushing the same agenda, but now adding other tools and projects into the mix. So outside of Anthropic, who continue to believe that the only safe models are closed models, everyone is now pushing for open AI (lots of nuances not really called out, open weights, openmodels, opensource all being conflated imo), but trying to also put a US spin on it. "Open Weights and
American AI Leadership" being the title of the open letter, that eventually OpenAI also signed, while continuing to lobby against Chinese models. 

So now the debate moves to the administration. If closed models are to be subjected to review before release, how is that done for open weight models, especially the ones that are non-US and how is distillation handled (IP theft is a term being banded around). The feeling being that Open is good as long as its US based (not chinese) and provably not distilled from closed US frontier models. The adminstration is due to have a proposal by the end of this week/early next week.

Its going to be fascinating to see how all this plays out. The US labs are now clearly in a vice, as is the administrations America First strategy.
